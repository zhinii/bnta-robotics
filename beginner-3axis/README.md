# Robot challenges · 机器人挑战

The default `beginner-3axis/` entry is a bilingual challenge-only robot workspace. It contains no lesson sequence, vocabulary tour, quiz, prediction prompt or reflection flow. All three tasks run locally in the browser without AI or an API key.

## Explore and three challenges

| Difficulty | Robot | Task |
|---|---|---|
| Explore | Three or six joints | Free play with three blocks. Pick up, move, put down and pick up again, with no objective or completion gate. |
| Easy | Three positioning joints | Pick up the part and place it using shared-world X/Y/Z coordinates. |
| Medium | Six joints | Guide the long part through a 132 × 132 mm square opening in a tall wall, then match the tilt of the destination cradle and release. |
| Hard | Two six-joint arms | A picks up the part, both arms align at the transfer marks, B closes its gripper, A opens its gripper, then B moves and places it. |

Six-axis tasks open with J1–J6 sliders and numerical angle fields. An XYZ helper preserves the tool's current orientation; wrist rotation remains necessary to fit the medium task. Typing XYZ coordinates or moving their sliders immediately displays a target cross and a ghost arm when a reachable pose exists. XYZ keeps the wrist attitude selected in the joint preview. An unreachable target remains marked in red with a bilingual explanation; it never silently disappears or enables Move. Preview does not move an arm, record a step or change who holds the part.

Explore, Easy, Medium and Hard are selectable from the first visit; no previous challenge is required. The robot selector in the scene header switches between the original three-axis and six-axis models; six-axis remains the default. Switching resets the scene and keeps separate programs for each robot during this visit. Save/Open files include the robot axes; older Explore files open with six axes. The three-axis robot starts with XYZ controls; the six-axis robot starts with all six joint controls and offers an XYZ preview helper. Blocks settle onto the work surface or another block when released, remain at their new positions, and can be picked up again. Their current coordinates stay visible. There are no task stands, success/failure targets or forced restarts. Collision and joint-limit feedback still apply; Record/Replay and portable program files are optional. Explore replay finishes normally without awarding challenge progress. Reset restores the three blocks while keeping the program.

## Simple programming

Select A or B before operating or adding a step. One ordered program labels every step with its arm; execution is sequential, so the two arms coordinate through explicit movement and handoff steps. This version does not run two independent programs simultaneously.

**Record** resets the scene, replaces the current program, and records each executed move, gripper action and handoff, including an action that drops the part. Target previews and rejected/interrupted actions are not recorded. **Finish**, then **Replay** beside Record. You can also add a previewed move and gripper action without executing it. Preview, reorder or remove rows. **Replay** checks the sequence, resets the scene, then animates it. In Hard, an unsupported release or a move while both arms grip produces a visible drop and a failed attempt. Playback reaches the offending action, drops the part, then stops with a bilingual explanation and Restart button. Failed actions do not increase move/action counts or complete the challenge. Pause and Stop are always available; Stop keeps the current pose and recorded steps; stopping an already falling part still leaves the attempt failed. Reset keeps the program.

The replay dashboard shows simulated execution time, completed/total moves and completed/total actions. Every action, including gripper changes and handoffs, contributes its model duration; pauses and thinking time are excluded. Timing is independent of display frame rate. Stopped, invalid and unfinished challenges are labelled separately from valid completed results. Each completed program row shows its own duration. Editing a program clears stale timing; exported JSON includes the latest replay result for assessment (import ignores claimed results and measures a fresh replay). Compare successful attempts within the same difficulty.

All challenges reuse the white/orange FAIRINO-inspired robot geometry from the main Robot Lab (`robot-arm-lab/src/scene.js`), extracted directly into `fairino-arm.mjs`: the original lathed shells, joint covers, screw patterns, pedestal, base plate and gripper. Both hard-mode arms use the same model. The adapter places it on the challenge joint poses; the preview uses the same geometry. Gripper and shell collision bounds follow the restored dimensions; the medium wall is 540 mm tall, with an upright 132 × 132 mm square opening. Each side matches the previous opening’s longest side (132 mm). The 112 mm part can pass at different orientations; there is no fixed passage-angle rule. The part, gripper and arm must clear the actual edges. The green destination cradle retains its 30° tilt. Convex wall panels and oriented-box collision checks share the rendered opening. The slot is at X 215, Y 0, Z 180 mm, with the pickup in front and destination behind. Going around or over the wall does not satisfy the task. Small decorative details remain visual approximations.

Manual placement is practice, never a challenge pass. Completion requires replay from the initial scene, every recorded step finishing, and all physical task conditions being met. Paused, stopped, incomplete or failed playback cannot pass. Editing, importing or restarting clears the current verified result. Successful replay marks only the current challenge complete; access to other difficulties is independent of completion.

Both three- and six-axis robots check every solid gripper member against all blocks, including held parts. The open space between the fingers remains usable for pickup; surface contact is allowed but penetration is not. Opening and closing check the full finger travel, including Explore releases. Movement checks intermediate poses, so a clear destination cannot bypass a collision along the route. Rejected motion leaves the robot, objects and recording unchanged; replay applies the same checks. Approach with open jaws and add a clearance waypoint when needed. Saved routes that previously passed through a block now report a collision instead.

Editing XYZ or joints previews the complete sampled movement using the same checks as Move. A blocked route shows a red ghost at the first blocked pose, a red dotted tool path ending there, and a red `!` marker near the contact. The affected block or wall is highlighted. The requested target stays marked separately. A clear route restores the blue preview and original object colors. Previewing never moves the live robot, records a step, or drops a shared handoff; previews are hidden during real movement. Contact markers show the detected collision region, not a calibrated physical contact point.

The iPad layout follows the reviewed no-Guide mockup. Navigation, language and Stop share the top bar. The scene header contains the Explore robot selector and a Task info disclosure; instructions can be reopened without resetting. Small numbered scene pins match the object/location strip below the scene. The original FAIRINO models are unchanged.

On touch devices, tapping an XYZ or joint value opens a compact number pad inside the controls panel. It replaces the normal controls while keeping the robot scene, current/planned positions and collision preview visible. Use the coordinate/joint chips, digits, decimal, sign, backspace and fine-adjust buttons to edit. Done keeps the preview; Cancel restores the complete draft from before opening. Neither action moves or records the robot. A Keyboard button explicitly enables native text entry; desktop fields keep direct typing, with a 123 button to open the same pad. The pad and its language/robot context reset with the current session. iPad layouts are verified with browser viewport emulation; native Safari keyboard behavior requires physical-device checking.

Controls separates Robot now and Planned position from editable XYZ/joint inputs. All six 44-pixel joint inputs fit at 1024×768 landscape and 768×1024 portrait, including Hard's separate Controlling Arm A/B selector. The Move now / Add program step buttons, Gripper group and Record footer have distinct roles. Previewing never moves the robot. Phone layouts use page scrolling instead of clipping controls into a fixed-height panel.

Finishing recording opens Program. The compact step list keeps detailed angles, preview, reorder and removal behind each row's options button. Playback/results, move/action counts, and Save/Open files live in Program. Actions include gripper commands; timing remains simulated execution time. A single status strip reports feedback. There is no Guide, chat or command input in this version; the earlier Guide modules remain separate legacy source.

Every page load or reload starts in Explore with the original block positions, empty programs, zero timers and no challenge progress. Language returns to English. Programs stay in memory only while this page is open, including when switching modes. The legacy `bnta:robot-challenges:v1` browser save is removed on startup; unrelated storage is untouched. Save/Open file exports and validates a portable JSON program so learners can keep work before reloading. Returning through the browser back/forward cache also starts fresh.

English and Chinese interface strings, task instructions and errors are paired in `challenge-i18n.mjs`. The simulator runs locally in the browser without AI or an API key.

## Run and verify

## Model and source boundaries

`challenge-model.mjs` owns state, sampled joint paths, arm/gate/table collision checks, gripper attachment, handoff and completion. `challenge-scene.mjs` renders that same geometry. `challenge-ui.mjs` owns the interface and simple program list.

Easy mode reuses the existing three-axis kinematics in `model.mjs`. `challenge-kinematics.mjs` is a self-contained copy of the established `six-axis-agent/kinematics.mjs`; the separate six-axis application is not edited. Both arms share one world frame and keep separate joint states.

This is an educational geometric simulation with a simplified falling animation, not a force, friction or impact solver or a hardware controller. Pickup clips the oriented part against the actual finger contact volume: it allows offsets and varied tool angles when both jaws can contact the part, without a fixed 3 mm pickup point or level-wrist requirement. Fingers may touch the graspable part during approach; palm, link, wall and other-arm collisions remain errors. Closing checks the finger sweep, and the visual jaws stop against the detected part surfaces. A missed Hard-mode close can close empty; it never attaches the part remotely. Placement accepts a small support tolerance. Handoff has separate recorded actions: B closes around a free section, then A opens. Releasing without support or moving while both grip drops the part and latches failure until a fresh attempt; B may open again to cancel its grip safely. Releasing A transfers ownership without teleporting the part, and B can then move. Both robots remain subject to collision checks. Older saved one-click handoff steps are expanded into B Close and A Open when loaded. Program completion comes from the actual challenge state, not from running a fixed number of steps.
